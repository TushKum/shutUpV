**Company:** AquaSense · **Ticker:** AQS

**Problem and target user**
In Tamil Nadu's small towns, drinking water sits in overhead tanks that panchayat staff check about once a month, by
eye. Contamination is found only after people fall ill. In Tiruvallur district last year, 2,300 people were treated for
waterborne illness from 41 tanks; each outbreak costs a family about ₹3,000 in treatment and lost wages. Our user is
the panchayat secretary who answers for the water: 12,500 panchayats in Tamil Nadu, most with 2–6 tanks.

**Proposed solution**
A ₹4,000 sensor box clamped inside each tank measures chlorine and turbidity every 15 minutes. When either goes out of
range, the panchayat secretary and the tank operator get an SMS in Tamil with what to do. We built a prototype from
off-the-shelf parts and ran it in two tanks for three weeks; it caught one chlorine failure that the monthly check
missed.

**Core features and MVP scope**
The MVP is the box, the SMS alert and a web page with each tank's history. Left out of the first version: an app, other
water tests and automatic dosing. The seed money pays for 300 boxes ($15,000), two field technicians for a year
($18,000), SMS and server ($4,000) and installation in 100 tanks across 25 panchayats ($13,000).

**Success metrics**
Within six months: 100 tanks live, alerts sent within 15 minutes of a failure, and no outbreak in a tank we monitor.
The metric that decides whether it works: failures caught before anyone falls ill.

**Business model**
₹1,500 per tank per month, billed quarterly to the panchayat from the Jal Jeevan Mission budget, with the sensor box
included. Each box costs us ₹4,000 to build and about ₹200 a month for SMS and the server, so a tank pays back its box
in three months. Two panchayats have given us letters of intent.

**Competitive edge**
Today panchayats choose between a monthly lab test (₹800, results in a week) or nothing. We give alerts within 15
minutes for less than two lab tests, and every tank we fit adds to the data on which tanks fail and when.

**Key risks**
Sensors drift in hard water: we recalibrate each box monthly on the technician's round. Payments from panchayats can be
late: we bill quarterly in advance from the scheme budget.
