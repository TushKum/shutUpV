# Case study B: CampusCart (CCRT), a good plan that the squad threw away

A squad with a good idea and a good plan that misses two deadlines. The numbers in **bold** are the platform's
acceptance tests; the others are illustrative.

| Squad 22 | Team | Code |
|---|---|---|
| Product | CampusCart | P22 |
| Consulting | Ridgeway Partners | C04 |
| Finance | Orbit Fund | F12 |

Problem card picked: **#30, Food: Grocery for hostel students.** CampusCart takes evening orders for fruit, milk and
basics from hostel students and delivers one batch per hostel at 08:00, buying from two local wholesalers.

## The pitch: 55, IPO $10.00

The pitch names the problem well, but "we will charge a small delivery fee" has no price and no volume, and the
advantage is "we are students too". Median **55 → 0% → IPO price $10.00**; the AI price starts at $10.00.

## Crisis: Partner exit, "Your biggest partner walks away"

The wholesaler supplying 80% of the stock ends the arrangement and will deliver to hostels itself. Both prices fall
15%; the AI price becomes $10.00 × 0.85 = **$8.50**. The market price, $9.40 after round 4, becomes $7.99: the post-crisis
price, so the deal band is $4.00–$7.99.

## 00:45–01:05 The fee: never agreed

Ridgeway proposes $25,000 cash. CampusCart wants to pay in shares and proposes $10,000 cash + 2,000 shares; Ridgeway
proposes again. Each proposal is a new version and needs **both** confirmations; at 01:05 the two teams have never
confirmed the same version.

At 01:05 the **default fee of $22,500 cash** moves automatically: CampusCart's cash $50,000 → **$27,500**. Neither team
chose this outcome.

## 01:05–03:00 The deal: signed, then unsigned

- 02:30 Ridgeway sets the price at $6.50; Orbit sets $50,000. CampusCart and Ridgeway sign.
- 02:45 The deal bonus cutoff passes: not fully signed, so **no $5,000 bonus** for Ridgeway.
- 02:58 Orbit changes the amount to $45,000 before signing. **Any edit clears every signature.**
- 03:00 Orbit has signed the new version; CampusCart and Ridgeway have not. **No signed deal.**

## 03:30 Verdicts: 78 becomes 50

The plan itself is good: a second wholesaler at 6% higher cost, a ₹10 price rise, three hostels kept and a cash table.
Its median is **78**. But the deal was not fully signed at 03:00, so **the score is capped at 50 → 0%**. With 78 it
would have been +10%.

Flash answer: 55 → 0%.

## The close

- AI price = $10.00 × 0.85 × (1 + 0%) × (1 + 0%) = **$8.50**
- Rounds 20 and 21 at $7.90 and $7.70 → market close **$7.80**
- **Closing price = ($7.80 + $8.50) ÷ 2 = $8.15**

| Team | Calculation | Final |
|---|---|---|
| CampusCart (Product) | 60,000 × $8.15 + $27,500 | **$516,500** |
| Ridgeway (Consulting) | $22,500 fee + $0 plan bonus (50 − 50) + no deal bonus + calls | $22,500 + calls |

## What it cost

- **Plan tier:** with 78 uncapped, the AI price alone would have been $8.50 × 1.10 = $9.35, lifting the closing price
  by $0.43 to at least $8.58, before the +10% the market price would also have received at 03:30.
- **Ridgeway's plan bonus:** $500 × (78 − 50) = $14,000, lost. Plus the $5,000 deal bonus.
- **The fee:** CampusCart's own offer, $10,000 cash + 2,000 shares (worth $30,000 at the $10.00 IPO price), would have
  cost more in value but kept $12,500 more cash; agreeing on any version would have let the squad choose its own mix.

## Lessons

1. **Agree the fee in the first ten minutes.** Propose once, then both confirm; don't trade proposals.
2. **Sign the deal by 02:30, not 02:59.** The 02:45 cutoff pays the consultant; 03:00 decides the plan cap.
3. **Never edit a deal after the others have signed**, unless all three of you are at the screen to sign again.
4. **Check the deal panel at 02:55**: it shows who has signed the current version.
