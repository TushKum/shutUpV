# Case study A: AquaSense (AQS), a rescue that works

A squad that does everything on time. Every number here is one of the platform's acceptance tests, so the game will
produce exactly these results.

| Squad 7 | Team | Code |
|---|---|---|
| Product | AquaSense | P07 |
| Consulting | Northline Consulting | C19 |
| Finance | Delta Capital | F33 |

Problem card dealt and picked: **#1, Water and environment: Unsafe water in small towns.**

## 21:00 The draw

The squad is formed. Delta buys AquaSense's **5,000 seed shares at $10.00**: AquaSense gets **$50,000** in cash, Delta
pays $50,000 and holds the 5,000 shares in its squad lot. AquaSense keeps **60,000** retained shares. Northline is
assigned two other companies to cover; AquaSense is covered by two other consultants, among them Peak Advisory.

## 21:10–22:30 The pitch

AquaSense writes a 280-word pitch: cheap sensors in overhead tanks that text the panchayat when chlorine or turbidity
goes out of range, sold as a ₹1,500/month subscription per tank. The full text is in
[samples/PITCH-strong.md](../samples/PITCH-strong.md).

The judge runs it three times: **64, 66, 68**. The spread is 4 (not more than 10), so no extra runs. **Median 66 → the
60–69 tier, +5% → IPO price $10.00 × 1.05 = $10.50.** The AI price starts at $10.50 too.

> Why not higher: the problem and solution were strong (about 20 each), but the business model gave a price without the
> cost of serving each tank, and the advantage ("first mover") is easy to copy.

## 22:30–23:30 Reading and IPO

Funds ask on the Q&A board: "Who pays when the panchayat has no budget?" AquaSense answers in 60 words (the district
Jal Jeevan Mission budget pays for testing; two panchayats have written letters of intent). Funds bid; the IPO clears
at $10.50.

Peak Advisory calls **BUY** on AQS for call 1 (baseline: the IPO price, $10.50).

## 23:30–00:30 Rounds 1–4

Funds buy. By the round 4 clearing AQS trades at **$11.25**. Peak's call 1 is judged: $11.25 > $10.50, **correct
(+$2,500 for Peak)**.

## 00:30 Crisis: Supply shortage, "A key input disappears"

The sensor chip AquaSense uses will be unavailable for 18 months; stock lasts six weeks.

| | Before | × 0.85 | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market price | $11.25 | | **$9.56** (the post-crisis price) |
| AI price | $10.50 | | **$8.93** |

The deal price band is now **50%–100% of $9.56 = $4.78–$9.56**.

## 00:45–01:05 The fee

Northline proposes **$20,000 cash + 1,000 shares**. Shares are valued at the IPO price: 1,000 × $10.50 = $10,500, so
the fee is worth **$30,500**: inside $10,000–$35,000, and under the 3,000-share limit. Both teams confirm at 00:58.

- AquaSense: cash $50,000 → $30,000; retained 60,000 → 59,000.
- Northline: cash $20,000; fee shares 1,000.

## 01:05–02:45 The deal

Northline proposes **$7.50** per share (78% of $9.56). Delta sets **$60,000**. shares = floor($60,000 ÷ $7.50) =
**8,000**; cash moved = 8,000 × $7.50 = $60,000. All three sign at 02:21, before the 02:45 cutoff, so Northline earns the
**$5,000 deal bonus**.

- AquaSense: cash $30,000 → **$90,000**; retained 59,000 → **51,000**.
- Delta: cash −$60,000; squad lot 5,000 → **13,000** shares (cost $50,000 + $60,000 = **$110,000**).

Why $7.50 and not $4.78? A cheaper price gives Delta more shares (12,552 at $4.78) but takes about 4,550 more shares from
AquaSense, and a deal at ≤55% of the post-crisis price with a weak plan raises a collusion flag. Why not $9.56? Delta
would get only 6,276 shares for the same money and might not sign; without a signed deal the plan is capped at 50.

## 02:00–03:00 The rescue plan

Product and Northline write the plan together (full text in [samples/PLAN-strong.md](../samples/PLAN-strong.md)):
switch to a second chip supplier with a three-week redesign, keep the six weeks of stock for the paying panchayats,
and show the cash month by month with the $60,000 deal. Submitted at 02:51.

Meanwhile the market drifts; at the round 12 clearing AQS is **$9.20**.

## 03:30 Verdicts

Plan runs **86, 88, 91** → median **88** → the 80–89 tier, **+20%**. The deal was signed in time, so there is no cap.

| | Before | × 1.20 | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market price | $9.20 | | **$11.04** |
| AI price | $8.93 | | **$10.72** |

Northline's plan bonus will be $500 × (88 − 50) = **$19,000**.

## 04:00–04:20 Flash news

The bulletin (see [flash bulletins](../../../seed/flash-bulletins/)) hits every company. AquaSense answers in 89 words
([samples/FLASH-strong.md](../samples/FLASH-strong.md)). Score **80** → **+5%**, applied after round 17 clears:

| | Before | × 1.05 | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market price | $12.50 | | **$13.13** |
| AI price | $10.72 | | **$11.26** |

The AI price is now final: $10.50 → $8.93 → $10.72 → $11.26.

## 05:00 The close

Rounds 20 and 21 clear at **$13.80** and **$14.20**.

- Market close = ($13.80 + $14.20) ÷ 2 = **$14.00**
- AI price = **$11.26**
- **Closing price = ($14.00 + $11.26) ÷ 2 = $12.63**

## Final values

| Team | Calculation | Final |
|---|---|---|
| AquaSense (Product) | 51,000 × $12.63 + $90,000 | **$734,130** |
| Northline (Consulting) | cash $20,000 + $19,000 plan bonus + $5,000 deal bonus + 4 correct calls × $2,500 = $54,000; plus 1,000 fee shares × $12.63 = $12,630 | **$66,630** |
| Delta (Finance), squad lot only | 13,000 × $12.63 = $164,190 against a cost of $110,000 | **+$54,190** on the lot |

Best turnaround for AQS: $12.63 ÷ $9.56 = 1.32×.

## What made the difference

1. **The plan score was worth more than anything else.** +20% on both prices; with a 45 instead it would have been −10%.
2. **The deal was signed early.** The plan was not capped, and the consultant got $5,000.
3. **The price was fair to both sides.** $7.50 gave Delta a discount without giving away AquaSense.
4. **The market overshot the AI price** ($14.00 vs $11.26). The closing price averages the two, so a strong plan and
   flash answer raise the floor for everyone holding the stock.
