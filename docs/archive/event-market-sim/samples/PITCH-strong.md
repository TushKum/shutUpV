**Company:** AquaSense · **Ticker:** AQS

**The problem**
In Tamil Nadu's small towns, drinking water sits in overhead tanks that panchayat staff check about once a month, by
eye. Contamination is found only after people fall ill. In Tiruvallur district last year, 2,300 people were treated for
waterborne illness from 41 tanks; each outbreak costs a family about ₹3,000 in treatment and lost wages.

**The solution**
A ₹4,000 sensor box clamped inside each tank measures chlorine and turbidity every 15 minutes. When either goes out of
range, the panchayat secretary and the tank operator get an SMS in Tamil with what to do. A web page shows every tank's
history. We built a prototype from off-the-shelf parts and ran it in two tanks for three weeks; it caught one chlorine
failure that the monthly check missed.

**Customers**
Village panchayats and town water boards: 12,500 panchayats in Tamil Nadu, most with 2–6 tanks. Testing is funded by
the Jal Jeevan Mission budget, which the district releases quarterly. Two panchayats have given us letters of intent.

**How we make money**
₹1,500 per tank per month, billed quarterly to the panchayat, with the sensor box included. Each box costs us ₹4,000 to
build and about ₹200 a month for SMS and the server, so a tank pays back its box in three months.

**Our advantage**
Today panchayats choose between a monthly lab test (₹800, results in a week) or nothing. We give alerts within 15
minutes for less than two lab tests. We are first in our district, and every tank we fit adds to the data that tells
us which tanks fail and when.

**Use of seed money**
$50,000: 300 sensor boxes ($15,000), two field technicians for a year ($18,000), SMS and server ($4,000), and
installation in 100 tanks across 25 panchayats ($13,000).
