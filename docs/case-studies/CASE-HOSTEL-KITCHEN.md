# Case: The Hostel Kitchen

**Market Simulation · case pack for contestants** · Product · Consulting · Finance

**Every squad plays this same case** from the draw to the close: the lottery deals it to all 50 squads, so there is no card to pick, and every company gets the same crisis. Read the whole pack with your squad before Round 1; the data
in the exhibits is all you need (and all the judges will expect you to use). Numbers are in rupees (₹) inside the
case; the game's share prices and cash are in virtual US dollars ($).

| Your squad | Track | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| The company | **Product** | Build the company that fixes this problem, write its PRD, survive the crisis, finish with the most value |
| The advisers | **Consulting** | Shape the PRD, earn a fee, write the rescue plan, make BUY/SELL calls on two other companies |
| The investor | **Finance** | Seed the company, invest in its rescue, and trade the whole market with $500,000 |

## 1. The situation

**Kaveri University**, on the edge of a large Indian city, houses **4,800 students in six hostels**. All six are fed by
one outside caterer, **Annapurna Mess Services**, on a contract that is renewed every March.

Every day the mess cooks for the number of students on its list. Many do not come: they sleep through breakfast, eat
out with friends, or skip a meal to save money. What is not eaten is scraped into bins and carted away. At the same
time, students who missed dinner spend ₹100–₹200 on late-night delivery, and the poorer ones simply go without.

The university's sustainability office has been told to cut food waste by half within a year. Annapurna has been
warned that its next contract depends on it. Nobody yet has a way to get the food that is cooked to the students who
want it.

**Your problem card (#2, Food):** *College hostels throw away a large share of the food they cook every day because
demand is hard to predict.*

## 2. Exhibits

### Exhibit 1 · The mess, by the numbers (a normal teaching week)

| | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plates cooked | 4,200 | 4,200 | 4,200 | **12,600** |
| Plates eaten | 2,900 | 3,500 | 3,300 | **9,700** |
| Plates wasted | 1,300 | 700 | 900 | **2,900 (23%)** |

- Cost to Annapurna of one cooked plate (ingredients, gas, staff): **₹42**.
- Food thrown away per day: about 2,900 plates × 0.45 kg = **1.3 tonnes**; disposal costs **₹1.50 per kg**.
- Annapurna is paid **₹3,150 per student per month**. If the university's waste audit finds more than **20%** waste,
  Annapurna pays a **₹50,000 penalty** per audit (audits are quarterly).

- Dinner is served 7:30–9:30 pm. Food left at 9:30 pm is thrown away by 10:30 pm.

### Exhibit 2 · Student survey (600 hostel residents, last month)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Skipped at least one mess meal last week | **61%** |
| Buy late-night food (after 9:30 pm) three or more times a week | **47%**, average spend **₹110** per order |
| Would buy a fresh mess-cooked meal box between 9:30 and 11 pm at **₹50** | **68%** yes · at ₹70: **41%** · at ₹90: **19%** |
| Biggest worry about eating leftover mess food | Hygiene **52%**, taste **24%**, being seen **9%**, none **15%** |
| How they pay for small purchases | UPI **92%**, cash **8%** |
| Would pick up from a counter at their own hostel | **83%** · would walk to another hostel: **31%** |

### Exhibit 3 · What things cost

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Compostable meal box with lid | ₹7 each |
| Insulated carrier (holds 20 boxes, keeps food above 60 °C for 90 min) | ₹1,800, lasts about two years |
| Student counter staff (part-time) | ₹120 per hour |
| Food-safety training and certificate, per kitchen | ₹6,000, once |
| Commercial chiller, 300 litres | ₹48,000, electricity about ₹40 a day |
| UPI payments | free |
| A simple ordering app or WhatsApp bot (build) | about ₹60,000, or ₹4,000 a month for a ready-made tool |

### Exhibit 4 · Alternatives students and the mess have today

| Option | What it costs | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Night canteen on campus | ₹90 a meal | Closes at 11 pm; one location, long queues |
| Delivery apps | ₹180 average with fees | 35–50 minutes; not allowed inside two hostels after 11 pm |
| "Roti Bank" NGO | Free | Collects surplus only on Sundays, for people outside campus |
| Cook less | – | Annapurna tried it in 2025: complaints and 3 days of shortages in two weeks |

### Exhibit 5 · Who decides

- **The university** (sustainability office and the chief warden) must approve anything sold inside the hostels.
- **Annapurna** owns the food until it leaves the kitchen, and is keen on anything that cuts the penalty and the
  disposal bill.

- **Hostel councils** (student-elected) control counter space and can promote, or kill, a new service in a week.

## 3. The night, round by round

The platform enforces every limit below; your screen always shows the real deadline.

### Round 1 · Build and IPO (21:00–00:30)

**Product: the PRD** (Product Requirements Document, ≤ **800 words**, submitted by Product, hard close **22:30**).
Use the seven sections:

1. **Problem and target user**: who suffers and how much, with numbers from Exhibits 1–2.
2. **Proposed solution**: what you sell, when, where and how it stays safe.
3. **Core features and MVP scope**: what the first version does in week 1 (and what it leaves out).
4. **Success metrics**: the 3–4 numbers that prove it works (boxes a day, waste %, repeat buyers, hygiene incidents).
5. **Business model**: price, cost and margin **per box**, boxes a day to break even, and what the $50,000 seed buys
   first.
6. **Competitive edge**: why a copycat cannot simply do this next term.
7. **Key risks**: what could go wrong (hygiene, Annapurna saying no, low demand) and what you would do about each.

Also choose a **company name** and a unique **3–4 letter ticker**.

**Consulting.** Read and improve the PRD in your squad's workspace (only Product submits). Then study your **two
assigned companies** (outside your squad) and make **call 1: BUY or SELL** on each by **23:15**: will the price after
trading round 4 be above its IPO price?

**Finance.** Read the PRD book (every company's PRD), then ask sharp questions on the public **Q&A board** (founders answer in ≤100
words). From **23:15 to 23:30** bid in the **IPO**: 0–4,000 shares per company, never your own squad's company, and
no more than your cash at IPO prices. A company whose bids exceed its 35,000 shares is scaled down.

*How the PRD sets the IPO price:* the judge's score (median of three readings) → **90–100 or 80–89: +10%**,
70–79 or 60–69: +5%, 50–59: 0, 40–49: −5%, below 40: −10% → **IPO price = $10.00 × (1 + tier)**.

| PRD rubric | Points |
|---|---|
| Problem and target user | 20 |
| Solution and MVP scope | 20 |
| Success metrics | 15 |
| Business model | 15 |
| Feasibility and risks | 15 |
| Clarity | 15 |

### Round 2 · Crisis and rescue (00:30–03:00)

At **00:30** trading halts and your crisis card arrives. Every company is hit; both its market price and its AI price
fall **15%**. That new market price is your **post-crisis price**. For this case, the crisis is:

> **Crisis (Regulation): the food-safety rule.** From next month, cooked food may leave a hostel kitchen only if it is
> **kept below 5 °C** until it is served, or **served within 2 hours** of cooking. The first inspection is in 30 days;
> a kitchen that fails is shut for a week. Annapurna's dinner is ready at 7:00 pm, so a 9:30 pm box is 2½ hours old.

**Product + Consulting: the fee** (agree by **01:05**). $10,000–$35,000, paid in cash and/or up to 3,000 of Product's
shares counted at the IPO price. Both teams confirm; otherwise a **$22,500 cash** default moves at 01:05.

**All three: the rescue deal** (sign by **02:45** for the consultant's $5,000 bonus; at the latest by 03:00).

- Consulting sets the **price**: 50%–100% of the post-crisis price.
- Finance sets the **amount**: $40,000–$80,000.
- Shares moved = amount ÷ price (rounded down). Any edit clears every signature.
- **No signed deal at 03:00 = the rescue plan is capped at 50.**

**Product + Consulting: the rescue plan** (≤ **500 words**, one shared editor, hard deadline **03:00**, late = 0).
Use the seven sections: **The crisis · The new plan · The money · The rescue deal · Time to recovery · Risks · Next
three steps.** The judge also reads your PRD, the crisis card and a summary of your deal.

| Plan rubric | Points |
|---|---|
| Solves the crisis | 25 |
| Money logic | 20 |
| Funding and deal | 20 |
| Time to recovery | 15 |
| New risks | 10 |
| Next steps | 10 |

**Finance** keeps trading (rounds 5–12, break 01:45–02:00). Long ≤4,000 shares per company, short ≤2,000 per company
and ≤$250,000 in total, buys reserve price × 1.10, shorts need 150% collateral.

### Round 3 · Verdicts, flash news and the close (03:00–05:40)

**03:00–03:30** every plan is published, without scores. **03:30** the verdicts: the plan tier moves **both** prices:
**90–100: +25%**, 80–89: +20%, 70–79: +10%, 60–69: +5%, 50–59: 0, 40–49: −10%, below 40: −20%.

**Consulting:** **call 2** by **03:40** (judged after the flash, 04:20) and **call 3** by **04:30** (judged at the close),
on your two assigned companies.

**04:00 Flash news.** For this case the bulletin is:

> **Flash: Cooking-gas cylinders up 25% from tonight.** The price of commercial LPG rises 25% for at least three
> months. Caterers, canteens and restaurants say meal prices will go up within a week.

**Product (or Consulting): the flash answer** (≤ **100 words**, by **04:15**): what changes in your business, in
numbers, and what you do about it this week. Tier at **04:20**: 70+: **+5%**, 50–69: 0, below 50: **−5%**.

| Flash rubric | Points |
|---|---|
| Responds to the news | 50 |
| Realistic | 30 |
| Clear | 20 |

**Finance** trades rounds 13–21 (10 minutes each). At **05:00** shorts are covered and the **closing price** is fixed:
**½ × (average of the round 20 and 21 prices) + ½ × AI price**. The AI price only moves with the judges' scores:
IPO price × 0.85 × (1 + plan tier) × (1 + flash tier).

## 4. How you win

| Track | Final value | Tie-break |
|---|---|---|
| Product | retained shares × closing price + cash | higher plan score |
| Consulting | cash (fee + plan bonus $500 × (plan − 50) + $5,000 deal bonus + $2,500 per correct call) + fee shares × closing price | higher plan score of your squad's company |
| Finance | cash + long shares × closing price − short shares × closing price, ranked on return on $500,000 | smaller largest single position |

Awards: top 3 in each track · best turnaround (closing ÷ post-crisis price) · best rescue plan · best-judging fund
(portfolio at AI prices only).

**Fair play.** Lines addressed to the judge are removed; a second offence costs 10 points. Collusion flags (funds piling
into a weak company, look-alike order books, sweetheart fees or deals) are reviewed by the faculty fairness officer.
